# Episode 002: The first number you see (vertical short, target 75 to 85 seconds)

Voice notes for ElevenLabs: tags in [brackets] are performance cues, kept light. Speak plainly and let the pauses do the work.

[curious] Here's a strange experiment.

Students at MIT were asked to write down the last two digits of their social security number. Just a random number. [pause]

Then they bid on things. A keyboard. Wine. Chocolate.

[softly] And that random number changed what they were willing to pay.

The students with the highest numbers offered about fifty-six dollars for the keyboard. The students with the lowest? About sixteen. [pause] For the same keyboard.

Psychologists call it anchoring. The first number you see becomes a starting point, and every judgment after it is adjusted from there. Usually not enough.

[curious] Now think about a store.

A three-hundred-dollar jacket hangs by the door. The hundred-and-twenty-dollar one beside it suddenly feels... reasonable.

A tag says "was two hundred, now one-twenty." [sighs] That crossed-out price isn't information. It's an anchor.

And here's the unsettling part. In that experiment, the anchor was completely random. It meant nothing. [pause]

[whispers] It still worked.

So next time the first number you see feels fair, ask one question: where did that number come from?

Because the first number isn't always the right one. [pause] It's just the one that got there first.
