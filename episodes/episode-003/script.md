# Episode 003: Why a mug you own is worth double (endowment effect)
Format: vertical 1080x1920, target 70-80 s, ElevenLabs tags kept light.

## Voiceover (paste into ElevenLabs)

[curious] Here's a coffee mug. Worth about three dollars.

Now I hand it to you. Same mug. [pause] Suddenly it's worth more.

[softly] That's not a trick. It's an experiment.

In 1990, economists gave coffee mugs to half the students in a room. Then they let everyone trade.

Owners were asked: what's the lowest price you'd sell for? Buyers were asked: what's the most you'd pay?

[pause] Owners wanted more than twice as much. In one run, the median owner asked seven dollars. The median buyer offered under three.

Same mug. Same room. The only difference was who was holding it.

And that kills the trade. If mugs were handed out at random, about half of them should have changed hands. Instead, only a handful did. In one early session, one in five.

[whispers] Nobody was being greedy. Owning it changed the price.

Psychologists call it the endowment effect. The moment something is yours, giving it up feels like a loss, and losses weigh more than gains.

That's why free trials work. That's why you keep the jacket you never wear.

[pause] Ask yourself: would you buy it today, at the price you're asking?
